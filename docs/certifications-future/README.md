# Certifications — future direction (research, do not publish)

**Status: NONE of the below are currently held.** These claims appeared on the site, gathered by mistake from other suppliers' sites, and were removed in the 2026-10-16 truthfulness sweep. Kept here as a list of directions Tündük *could* grow into, if and when we commission real testing. Nothing in this folder may go back on the site until it is genuinely earned and documented.

## What we actually hold today
- **Certified fumigation** — a real export/phytosanitary document, part of international shipping. (Claimable, on the site.)
- **UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage** — the Kyrgyz yurt-making *craft* is on UNESCO's Representative List (2014). This is about the tradition, not a product certification. (Claimable.)
- **Ministry of Culture (Kyrgyz Republic)** — cultural preservation of the craft. (Claimable as heritage, not as a product standard.)
- Our own QC/approval process, component dimensions, materials spec, assembly methodology, Adylet's recorded walkthrough.

## Certifications to research (potential future investment)
Each would require commissioning accredited testing / a licensed professional. None is a claim we can make now.

| Cert | What it covers | Who issues / how to get it |
|---|---|---|
| **ASTM E84** | Surface burning characteristics (flame spread / smoke) of interior finishes — US interior occupancy | Accredited fire-test lab, on samples of the felt/covers/linings |
| **NFPA 701** | Flame propagation of textiles and films — drapery/fabric in public occupancies (US) | Accredited lab test on the fabrics |
| **CPAI-84** | Flammability of tent fabrics (US camping/assembly tents) | Accredited lab test on tent fabric |
| **Eurocode (EN)** | EU structural design (incl. wind/snow loads) + EU reaction-to-fire classes (EN 13501-1) | Licensed structural engineer + EU-notified fire-test lab |
| **Structural certification** | Wind/snow load engineering for permits | Licensed engineer in the destination jurisdiction |
| **Fire-retardant textile treatment** | Treating felt/covers to pass the above fire tests | Specialist treatment + re-test (note: treatment changes a natural wool product — weigh against the "authentic, untreated" positioning) |

## Open questions for the research session
1. Which market needs which cert first (US vs EU vs UAE/Gulf hospitality procurement)?
2. Does fire-retardant treatment compromise the natural-wool, no-synthetics story? Is there an untreated route that still passes?
3. Cost and lead time of lab testing vs. the volume of orders that actually need it.
4. Can a buyer's own engineer certify a specific installation from our data pack, avoiding us holding blanket certs?

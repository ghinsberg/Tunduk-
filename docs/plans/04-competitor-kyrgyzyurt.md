# Competitor teardown — KyrgyzYurt (Etsy)

Source: etsy.com/shop/KyrgyzYurt + listing 739598641, read 2026-06-12. Internal.

## What they are
- Etsy shop, seller "Mansur Abylaev", registered Illinois US, **ships from Kyrgyzstan**.
- **Sources from the SAME place we do: the Kyzyl-Tuu village cooperative** (their "Maker" member is literally "Kyzyl Tuu village cooperative").
- Three listings only: 4m/12.5m² (€3,429), 5m/19m² (€4,332), 6m/28m² (€6,137). Sizing by lattice/kanat, same system as ours.
- **0 sales. 0 reviews. 134 admirers. Announcement last updated Jan 2021, policies Dec 2020.** Looks authoritative, is effectively dormant and untested.
- Team shown: Kyzyl Tuu cooperative (maker), Altynai (customer service), Timur (owner).

## The single biggest takeaway
The "category leader" on the obvious channel has **zero traction and sources from our village**. The authority position is empty. Our B2B-authority play is aimed at a real gap, not a crowded field.

## The category's fatal friction: shipping
On the 6m listing, **shipping = €4,061 on a €6,137 yurt** (~66% of product cost), single-unit, buyer handles all import customs. This is why single-unit Etsy yurt selling barely converts. **Our entire B2B model is the answer to this**: consolidated freight, all-in DAP, volume economics, installation team on site. We should make that contrast louder.

## Worth borrowing — PROCEDURES (mostly verify-then-add)
1. **Per-size spec sheet with part counts.** Their listing itemises: 1 Tündük, 85 Uuk, 6 Kerege, 1 Boosogo, Chiy, Uzuk, Tuurduk, plus tassel decor (chayan chachy, kerege chachy). B2B buyers want hard numbers. Add a size→(kanat, uuk count, m², approx weight) table to trade/faq. (Our 85-uuk figure already matches theirs.)
2. **"What's included" itemised box list.** Exactly what ships, incl. spare poles + bakan (centre prop) + assembly. Kills buyer uncertainty.
3. **Concrete cold-climate numbers + two-layer felt option.** Theirs: one layer 0.8–1cm rated to -20/-30°C, two layers to -40, usable to -50; 5m heats in 30–40 min with a small stove. The **two-layer felt roof for extreme cold** is a real product option we don't currently offer; note it doubles roof weight and needs a strengthened frame. Verify the numbers with Adylet before publishing.
4. **Wood seasonality detail.** They specify willow cut in late autumn/winter, debarked, wind-dried, then steam-treated, oiled, treated with natural insect repellent. Adds temporal specificity to our craft page. Minor, nice.
5. **FOB-at-airport option alongside DAP.** They offer FOB (buyer's own freight) or DAP door-to-door via FedEx/DHL. Our FAQ already leads with DAP; adding "or FOB if you have your own forwarder" suits trade buyers with logistics.
6. **Install/licensing nudge.** They tell buyers to check local municipality for yurt installation licensing over a certain footprint. We have permits FAQ; good to keep that helpfulness.

## Worth borrowing — COPY ANGLES (paraphrase, don't lift)
- They lean on "**authentic**" + "**heart of Central Asia**" + traditional gender division of labour (men build frame, women make/decorate felt) as authenticity signals. We already do heritage better; the gendered-craft detail is a true, human texture we could weave into the craft page tastefully.
- Their QC line is essentially ours: assembled, inspected, **inspection video sent before packaging**. Confirms video-QC is table stakes in this category, keep ours prominent (we already do).

## Where we are already stronger (protect these)
- **B2B volume vs their single-unit Etsy.** Their model dies on shipping; ours is built for it.
- **Named partners with real credentials** (Adylet, the cooperative, Yossi/Jusup) vs their generic team.
- **Installation team on site** vs them shipping a flat-pack.
- **Natural materials claim.** Note: their felt is dyed with "powdered paints of Russian production" and the cover is **water-resistant vinyl**. We claim natural plant dyes and breathable membrane. That is a genuine differentiator IF true, VERIFY with Adylet that our dyes/covers are actually natural, or soften our claim.
- **A living, coherent site** vs their stale, contradictory listing.

## What to AVOID (their mistakes)
- **Self-contradiction.** Announcement: "$4,800, delivery 1–3 weeks"; listing: 6–8 week production, €6,137; returns "not accepted" on the listing but "we gladly accept returns" in policies. Inconsistency erodes trust. Keep our numbers consistent (we reconciled 5–25 units / 6 months / pricing-by-quote across the spine).
- **Dormancy.** A polished listing with no upkeep = 0 sales in 6 years. Keep the site alive and dated.
- **Vinyl + non-natural dyes** marketed next to "authentic" is a tension. Don't borrow the vinyl framing; it undercuts the natural story.

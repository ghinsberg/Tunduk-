The Boz Üy

What exactly is a Boz Üy, is it the same as a Mongolian ger?

A Boz Üy is the Kyrgyz tent, "grey house" in Old Turkic. From a distance it reads like the Mongolian ger. Up close it is a different piece of architecture. The Kyrgyz roof poles, the uuk, are curved. They spring from the crown ring and carry the whole roof down into the lattice walls, so the dome stands on nothing but itself.

The ger uses straight poles and two posts planted in the middle of the floor. A Boz Üy has no posts, and no centre at all. The room is one unbroken circle, which is the difference you feel the moment you step inside.

What are the yurts made from, are any synthetic materials used?

Nothing synthetic. Every part is natural, and most of it grew within sight of the workshop.

  • Frame: hand-shaped willow (Turpan Tal), with birch
  • Bindings: rawhide, tied wet and shrunk tight. No nails, no screws
  • Walls: woven reed mats (chiy) around the lattice
  • Cover: handmade sheep-wool felt, thirteen rolls to a Boz Üy
  • Floor: shyrdak felt carpets, dyed by hand

One exception, and it is optional: a breathable rain cover for wet climates. It is the only synthetic thing we make, and it is an add-on, never part of the real structure.

How much does a fully assembled yurt weigh?

A 6m (20ft) Boz Üy weighs about 150 kg (330 lbs) standing. That is light: a comparable Mongolian ger runs 230 to 900 kg, a Western engineered yurt often over 1,000 lbs once crated. The weight is why a Boz Üy ships for less, clears customs more simply, and can be taken down and moved again if your plans change.

What does the tündük crown ring do, why is it significant?

The tündük is the wooden crown at the very top, the ring every roof pole runs into. It does the structural work, gathering the load of the whole roof into one circle. It also does the other kind of work: it is the most sacred part of the Boz Üy, the sun and the sky and the family's line beneath it. It sits at the centre of the Kyrgyz flag, ringed by forty rays for the forty tribes.

Most days it is left open to the light and the weather. A felt cap, raised and lowered on a rope from inside, closes it against rain or cold.

What is UNESCO intangible cultural heritage, and why does it matter?

UNESCO's Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage is how the world marks living traditions worth keeping, the crafts, songs and rituals that carry a people's knowledge. To be inscribed is to be judged significant to all of us, not to one country alone.

The knowledge and skills of making Kyrgyz yurts were inscribed in 2014. This is not a museum label. It is a living craft, still practised by hand in villages like Kyzyl-Tuu, and when you commission a Boz Üy from us, you commission it from the people who hold that knowledge.

How long does a Kyrgyz yurt last?

Cared for, a Boz Üy lasts twenty to fifty years. The willow frame, kept dry, is the part that endures. The felt works harder and wears sooner: fifteen to twenty years in a dry climate, and comparable under a breathable rain cover in a wet one.

And nothing fails all at once. A single felt panel, one uuk pole, the tündük cap, each can be replaced on its own. That is not a policy we invented. It is how nomad families have kept a Boz Üy alive for generations.

What floor area does each size provide?
  • 5m (16 ft): ~19.6 m² (211 sq ft). A studio, a meditation room, an intimate stay.
  • 6m (20 ft): ~28.3 m² (304 sq ft). Room to live and sleep, or a yoga floor.
  • 7m (23 ft): ~38.5 m² (414 sq ft). A family, or a workshop.
  • 8m (26 ft): ~50.3 m² (541 sq ft). An event space, a resort's centrepiece.

Larger still by commission: pole-free to 10m, and 11 to 12m with discreet central support. And because nothing stands in the middle, every square metre is usable, which is not true of a ger.

What sizes and finishes do you offer?

Four sizes, named the Kyrgyz way:

  • Jailoo (5m): the summer high pasture. Light, close, intimate.
  • Kochuu (6m): the migration. Our most-ordered size, poised between snug and generous.
  • Ala-Too (7m): the mountain range. Built for presence.
  • 8m and larger: a centrepiece, to order.

Then one real choice, the finish. Ak is undyed felt in cream and stone: the clean, architectural reading we recommend for hospitality, where the form carries the room and nothing is lifted out of its context. Oyu is the fully dressed Boz Üy, shyrdak carpets, oimo chiy mats, zabık bash bands, a carved bosogo door, for those who want exactly that, and we build it gladly. For wet climates we add a breathable rain cover and extra insulation. Every order is priced on a call, against your spec and your destination.

How does the ordering process work?

It starts with a conversation, not a cart.

Shipping & Delivery

Where do you ship to?

Anywhere on Earth. Freight cost and lead time vary by destination, so tell us where in your enquiry and we include it in the quote.

How long does shipping take?

Transit times depend on destination and method:

  • Air freight (express): 10 to 14 days to most destinations. Higher cost, suitable for time-sensitive projects.
  • Ocean freight (consolidated): 6 to 12 weeks to Europe, 8 to 14 weeks to North America and Australia. Most economical for larger orders.
  • Overland and ocean: Kyrgyzstan is landlocked, so ocean shipments route overland (via China or Central Asian rail corridors) to a seaport before ocean transit.

Lead time from order to delivery, production plus transit, is typically 14 to 20 weeks for ocean freight, or 10 to 14 weeks for air.

What are the Incoterms, who handles customs clearance?

We quote on DAP (Delivered at Place) terms as standard, meaning the price includes all freight, insurance, and delivery to your address. You are responsible for import duties and VAT on receipt.

We provide all required export documents: commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and phytosanitary treatment documentation for any wooden packaging (ISPM 15 compliant). We can also provide HS code guidance to help you estimate import duties in your country.

If your team would rather run its own freight forwarder, we can ship FOB from the airport with the full export documents, and you arrange carriage from there. Otherwise DAP, delivered to your door, is the standard.

How is the yurt packaged for shipping?

Components are grouped into labelled packages by category (frame, felt, reed mats, door, accessories). Wooden components are wrapped in protective film. Felt rolls are sealed in moisture-resistant bags. All packages are numbered, photographed, and matched to a packing list you receive before shipment.

A 6m yurt typically ships as 6 to 9 packages totalling approximately 165 to 200 kg gross weight and 1.8 to 2.4 CBM.

What happens if something is damaged in transit?

All shipments include all-risk cargo insurance. In the event of transit damage, photograph everything on receipt before signing off with the courier, and notify us within 48 hours with photos. We manage the insurance claim and replace damaged components. Because individual components are replaceable, a partial damage claim does not mean a full yurt replacement.

Do I need to be present for delivery?

We recommend being present or arranging a representative, as packages are heavy and should be inspected for transit damage on receipt. Your freight forwarder coordinates a delivery window in advance. For business deliveries, loading dock or forklift access may be needed for larger orders.

Weather & Climate

Can a Kyrgyz yurt handle rain? Will the felt get wet?

Traditional felt is designed for Central Asia's dry continental climate. In a wet climate (UK, Pacific Northwest, much of Europe), wool felt absorbs moisture and takes time to dry, manageable but not ideal for permanent structures. Our solution is the export breathable rain cover: a high-quality breathable membrane that sits over the felt, repelling liquid water while still allowing the wool to breathe and regulate humidity naturally.

The breathable membrane is strongly recommended for any yurt in a climate with more than ~600mm annual rainfall. It is standard in the export configuration and available as an add-on otherwise.

How cold can it get inside in winter?

Wool felt is a remarkable natural insulator. Kyrgyz families have lived in yurts through harsh Central Asian winters, down to -30°C (-22°F). Without active heating, the yurt equilibrates with outdoor temperatures over time. For year-round comfort we recommend a modern air-conditioning unit, a heat pump that both heats and cools, efficient and silent, with no open flame near felt and willow. With climate control and enhanced insulation, a Boz Üy is comfortable year-round in most European or North American climates.

For genuinely extreme cold, the roof can be built with a second layer of felt, which raises the rating considerably. A double-layer roof adds significant weight to the top of the structure, so we strengthen the frame to match, and we confirm the felt build to your climate before production.

For winter residential use we recommend the export configuration with enhanced insulation and an air-conditioning unit rated for the interior volume.
How does the yurt perform in strong wind?

The dome shape is aerodynamically efficient; it generates very little wind resistance compared to flat-sided structures. Tension bands hold the frame together, and the felt covering is secured with additional straps. Traditional yurts have withstood steppe winds for millennia. In coastal or exposed locations, we recommend anchoring the base to a platform and adding ground pegs for the outer tension straps. We provide specific guidance in the assembly guide.

Can yurts handle snow loads?

The curved roof naturally sheds snow, preventing accumulation. Moderate snow loads, typical for most European or North American winters, are handled well by the dome geometry. For regions with very heavy snowfall, we recommend keeping the interior heated so some warmth reaches the roof surface and prevents ice buildup around the tündük seal.

Is the yurt suitable for hot climates?

Yes. The tündük functions as a natural chimney. When the ring is open, hot air rises and exits through the top while cooler air is drawn in through the lower walls. This passive ventilation keeps interiors significantly cooler than the outside on hot days, and wool insulates in both directions. Many of our clients use yurts in hot Mediterranean, Middle Eastern, and Australian climates successfully.

How do I care for and maintain the felt?

Wool felt care is straightforward:

  • Allow felt to dry thoroughly if it gets wet, ventilate the interior
  • Brush off debris with a soft brush before storage
  • Store felt rolls in a dry location if the yurt is not in use for extended periods
  • Check for moth damage annually and treat with appropriate natural repellents
  • Individual felt panels can be replaced without replacing the full set

A full care and maintenance guide is included with every order.

Assembly & Installation

How long does it take to assemble a yurt?

A complete home, raised in a day. Skilled hands can race one up far faster, but placing a Boz Üy well is not a race. Allow half a day to a day to raise it carefully and dress it right. The structure needs no tools; the components slot, pin, and tie together. We support your builders with a filmed build and live video, and for larger projects our own experts come to your site to raise the Boz Üys with your team.

Do I need a foundation or platform?

Traditionally, yurts are erected directly on cleared level ground. For permanent or semi-permanent installations, a low raised timber platform (decking) is strongly recommended for:

  • Moisture isolation between the ground and the wooden frame
  • A level, clean floor surface
  • Improved insulation from ground cold
  • Easier compliance with local planning requirements

A simple timber platform can be built by any competent carpenter. We provide recommended specifications. No concrete foundations are required.

Do you provide an assembly guide and support?

Yes. Every order includes a full illustrated assembly guide and a recorded video walkthrough by Adylet, our master builder. All components are numbered and labelled to match the guide. We offer remote assembly support by video call during your first assembly at no additional charge, and on most projects our own team installs on site.

Can the yurt be moved after assembly?

Yes, by design. The yurt can be disassembled, transported, and re-erected an unlimited number of times. This is how nomadic families have used them for centuries. Components are numbered and pack consistently, so disassembly and re-packing is straightforward after the first time.

Can I add electricity, plumbing, or heating?

Yes to all. Many clients run their yurts as year-round holiday lets or studios with full services:

  • Electricity: cabling is typically run under the platform floor and enters through a discreet opening in the kerege base. LED strip lighting around the kerege and tündük is common.
  • Heating and cooling: a modern air-conditioning unit (a heat pump) is the efficient, fire-safe choice, and heats and cools in one. Electric underfloor heating in the platform is also popular.
  • Plumbing: not typically inside the yurt itself, but a separate bathroom adjacent to the yurt is a common configuration.
How many people does a 6m yurt sleep?

A 6m yurt (28.3 m²) comfortably sleeps 2 to 4 people as a holiday-let unit with a double or king bed, a small seating area, and storage. Configured as a family camping unit, up to 6 people can sleep on floor mats in traditional Kyrgyz style. For yoga or meditation, the 6m comfortably holds 8 to 12 participants.

Planning & Permits

Do I need planning permission to put up a yurt?

Regulations vary significantly by country, region, and intended use. The key variables are whether the yurt is on your own land, whether it is for personal or commercial use, how long it remains in place, and whether it is classified as a temporary or permanent structure.

General guidance (always verify with your local authority):

  • UK: temporary structures on private land for up to 28 days typically do not require planning permission. Permanent or commercial holiday-let installations often require consent.
  • US: varies by state and county. Many rural and agricultural zones permit yurts without a permit below a certain floor area.
  • EU: varies by country and municipality. Germany and France tend to require permits for structures above ~20 m².
  • Australia and NZ: similar to the EU, check with your local council.
We maintain a country-by-country guidance document and include the relevant section in your quote pack.
Can the yurt be used for commercial holiday lets or short-term rental?

Yes. This is one of our primary use cases. Many Tündük clients operate holiday lets, eco-resorts, and short-term rentals with our yurts. For commercial use we recommend the export configuration with the rain cover and climate control (air conditioning). Trade buyers should also check local hospitality licensing and fire safety requirements, which vary by jurisdiction; we can advise based on your location.

What should I know about fire safety?

Felt is a natural wool textile, not a treated flame-retardant fabric, and we make no fire-rating claims. For any commercial or guest use, follow your local fire-safety requirements and consult the relevant authority. We can provide material and component details for your assessment, and we never recommend leaving open flames unattended inside any fabric structure.

Can I get building or structural certification for a Tündük yurt?

We provide a data pack with component dimensions, materials specification, and assembly methodology. Formal structural engineering certification for permit purposes must be obtained from a licensed engineer in your jurisdiction. Some of our trade clients have had their yurt structures independently certified, and we provide the documentation they need. Contact us if you need this for a specific project.

How do I work out what size yurt I need?

A useful starting point:

  • Solo or couple retreat, garden studio: 5m (Jailoo)
  • One or two person guest unit, yoga studio for 8 to 12: 6m (Kochuu)
  • Family of four holiday let, workshop, small event: 7m (Ala-Too)
  • Resort centrepiece, large event, commercial space: 8m

Remember that 100% of the circular floor area is usable; there are no centre poles reducing your effective space. A 6m yurt's 28 m² is all genuinely open floor.

Kyrgyz Terminology

What does "tündük" mean, and why is it your name?

Tündük is the crown ring at the apex of a Kyrgyz yurt, the circular wooden hoop through which all roof poles (uuk) radiate outward and downward to the walls. Structurally, it holds the entire dome together. Symbolically, it represents the cosmos, the sun, and the unity of the family beneath it.

It is so central to Kyrgyz identity that the tündük sits at the centre of the national flag, a red field with a yellow tündük wheel. We chose the name because the tündük is simultaneously the most structurally critical element and the most symbolically loaded. Everything radiates from it.

What is a "kerege", and what does "6 kanat" mean?

The kerege is a folding lattice wall panel, the accordion-like wooden grid that forms the cylindrical base of the yurt. Each panel folds flat for transport and expands to form a section of the wall circle.

Kanat means "wing" in Kyrgyz, and it is the traditional unit for specifying yurt size. A "6 kanat" yurt has six kerege wall panels forming its perimeter. Our sizing follows this system: the 5m yurt is 4 kanat, the 6m is 6 kanat, the 7m is 8 kanat. The number of uuk roof poles follows proportionally: 4 kanat is 55 uuk, 6 kanat is 85 uuk, 8 kanat is 100 uuk. This is how Kyrgyz builders have specified yurts for centuries.

What are "uuk", how are they different from regular rafters?

Uuk are the curved roof poles that radiate from the tündük crown ring down to the top of the kerege walls, creating the domed roof. Unlike the straight rafters used in modern Western yurts, uuk are curved, steam-bent from Turpan Tal willow over 15 to 20 days so that each pole holds its arc permanently without mechanical assistance.

This curvature is what makes centre poles unnecessary. The dome holds itself through the geometry of compression: the uuk push outward at the kerege and upward into the tündük, creating a self-supporting structure. A 6m yurt carries 85 uuk, each cut and bent individually by hand.

What is "Turpan Tal" and why does it matter?

Turpan Tal is the specific species of willow used for all Kyrgyz yurt frames, harvested from river valleys in the Fergana and Talas regions. It is exceptionally light for its strength, naturally flexible when steamed, and resistant to splitting under tension once dried.

Not all willow is Turpan Tal. The species is selected specifically because it bends the way the uuk and kerege need to bend, holding a curve without cracking when the fibres are stressed by steam. Builders in Kyzyl-Tuu have used Turpan Tal for generations. Any other species produces a structurally inferior frame. It is one of the reasons authentic Kyrgyz yurts from the right villages are genuinely different from imitations.

What is "kiyiz", is it the same as felt?

Kiyiz is the Kyrgyz word for felt, specifically the handmade wool felt produced using traditional wet-felting techniques. It is not the same as commercial or industrial felt. Kiyiz is made by washing raw sheep wool, layering it on a reed mat, drenching it with hot water, then rolling and pressing it repeatedly, a process that takes a full day per panel and is traditionally done communally, since the rolling requires multiple people working in rhythm.

A standard 6m yurt uses thirteen rolls of kiyiz: eight for the roof, five for the walls. The felt is 2 to 4cm thick depending on the layer and is the primary insulation, weatherproofing, and structural skin of the yurt. Unlike synthetic covers, kiyiz self-regulates humidity and temperature; it absorbs moisture when the air is damp and releases it as the air dries.

What is "gök" binding, why no nails?

Gök is rawhide, strips of untanned animal hide applied wet to every joint in the kerege lattice. As rawhide dries, it contracts and hardens, creating a joint of extraordinary compressive strength. There are no nails, no screws, no metal fasteners of any kind in an authentic Kyrgyz yurt frame.

This is not a limitation, it is a structural advantage. Gök-bound joints flex under wind load and movement rather than cracking under stress the way rigid metal-fastened joints do. The yurt is designed to move slightly, distributing force across the entire structure rather than concentrating it at fixed points. This is why traditional yurts survive the extreme wind conditions of Kyrgyz mountain passes.

What is a "shyrdak", how is it different from "ala kiyiz"?

Both are handmade Kyrgyz felt carpets, made using completely different techniques.

Shyrdak is made by the mosaic appliqué method: two contrasting-colour felt layers are cut simultaneously with the same pattern, then the cut-out pieces are swapped and stitched together. The design is first drawn on the felt, then cut, then reassembled. Every motif is intentional and carries meaning, the ram's horn (strength), the crow's claw (lineage), the horn scroll (protection).

Ala kiyiz is older and made by the rolling method: coloured wool designs are laid into raw, unfelted wool, and then the entire surface is wet-rolled together, permanently fusing the design into the fabric as the fibres bond. Unlike shyrdak, you cannot plan an ala kiyiz on paper; the entire design must exist in the maker's memory before the wool is touched, because once rolling begins, the pattern cannot be altered.

What are "chiy" and "oimo chiy"?

Chiy are woven reed mats that wrap around the kerege walls beneath the felt covering. They serve as an essential insulation and ventilation layer, separating the felt from direct contact with the lattice, creating an air gap, and providing additional thermal mass. In a properly built Boz Üy, you never skip the chiy layer.

Oimo chiy is the premium version: during the weaving process, coloured wool is woven into the reed surface in geometric patterns. The decoration is not applied afterward, it is integral to the structure. Plain chiy serves function; oimo chiy serves function and beauty at once. It lines the interior of the kerege and is visible as a decorative band around the lower walls.

What are "zabık bash" and "bosogo"?

Zabık bash are the decorative felt bands that ring the yurt at the wall-to-roof junction. The ichki (inner) zabık bash lines the interior where the kerege meets the uuk, an embroidered felt strip with traditional motifs in red, green, and gold. The syrtky (outer) zabık bash runs around the exterior lower edge. Together they are the finishing detail that transforms a functional yurt into a fully dressed one. A yurt without zabık bash is like a suit without its buttons.

Bosogo is the wooden door frame, typically the most visually prominent element of the yurt exterior. In the natural Ak finish the bosogo is plain-painted; in the decorated Oyu finish it is hand-carved with ancestral motifs and painted in traditional colours by the master craftsman. The bosogo is what a guest sees first when approaching the yurt.

What do Jailoo, Kochuu, and Ala-Too mean, and what are Ak and Oyu?

Our three sizes are named for Kyrgyz words that carry meaning relevant to how and where each yurt lives:

  • Jailoo (5m): the summer alpine pasture, where nomadic families have pitched their yurts every July and August for thousands of years. Light, intimate, made for retreat.
  • Kochuu (6m): the migration, the act of moving your home to follow the seasons. Our most-ordered size. Balanced between intimacy and generosity.
  • Ala-Too (7m): the mountain range that rings Bishkek and defines the Kyrgyz skyline. Grand, commanding, built for permanence.

Within each size, the two finishes are:

  • Ak (white, pure): natural undyed kiyiz felt in cream and stone tones. No chemical treatment. The traditional palette of the steppe.
  • Oyu (motif, ornament): fully decorated with shyrdak carpets, oimo chiy reed mats, zabık bash felt bands, and a carved bosogo door frame. Everything handmade by the women and craftsmen of Kyzyl-Tuu.

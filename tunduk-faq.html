The Boz Üy

What exactly is a Boz Üy, is it the same as a Mongolian ger?

A Boz Üy is the Kyrgyz tent, "grey house" in Old Turkic. From a distance it reads like the Mongolian ger. Up close it is a different piece of architecture. The Kyrgyz roof poles, the uuk, are curved. They spring from the crown ring and carry the whole roof down into the lattice walls, so the dome stands on nothing but itself.

The ger uses straight poles and two posts planted in the middle of the floor. A Boz Üy has no posts, and no centre at all. The room is one unbroken circle, which is the difference you feel the moment you step inside.

What are the yurts made from, are any synthetic materials used?

Nothing synthetic. Every part is natural, and most of it grew within sight of the workshop.

  • Frame: hand-shaped willow (Turpan Tal), with birch
  • Bindings: rawhide, tied wet and shrunk tight. No nails, no screws
  • Walls: woven reed mats (chiy) around the lattice
  • Cover: handmade sheep-wool felt, thirteen rolls to a Boz Üy
  • Floor: shyrdak felt carpets, dyed by hand

One exception, and it is optional: a breathable rain cover for wet climates. It is the only synthetic thing we make, and it is an add-on, never part of the real structure.

How much does a fully assembled yurt weigh?

A 6m (20ft) Boz Üy weighs about 150 kg (330 lbs) standing. That is light: a comparable Mongolian ger runs 230 to 900 kg, a Western engineered yurt often over 1,000 lbs once crated. The weight is why a Boz Üy ships for less, clears customs more simply, and can be taken down and moved again if your plans change.

What does the tündük crown ring do, why is it significant?

The tündük is the wooden crown at the very top, the ring every roof pole runs into. It does the structural work, gathering the load of the whole roof into one circle. It also does the other kind of work: it is the most sacred part of the Boz Üy, the sun and the sky and the family's line beneath it. It sits at the centre of the Kyrgyz flag, ringed by forty rays for the forty tribes.

Most days it is left open to the light and the weather. A felt cap, raised and lowered on a rope from inside, closes it against rain or cold.

What is UNESCO intangible cultural heritage, and why does it matter?

UNESCO's Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage is how the world marks living traditions worth keeping, the crafts, songs and rituals that carry a people's knowledge. To be inscribed is to be judged significant to all of us, not to one country alone.

The knowledge and skills of making Kyrgyz yurts were inscribed in 2014, and in 2025 the listing widened to include the Karakalpak yurt too. This is not a museum label. It is a living craft, still practised by hand in villages like Kyzyl-Tuu, and when you commission a Boz Üy from us, you commission it from the people who hold that knowledge.

How long does a Kyrgyz yurt last?

Cared for, a Boz Üy lasts twenty to fifty years. The willow frame, kept dry, is the part that endures. The felt works harder and wears sooner: fifteen to twenty years in a dry climate, and comparable under a breathable rain cover in a wet one.

And nothing fails all at once. A single felt panel, one uuk pole, the tündük cap, each can be replaced on its own. That is not a policy we invented. It is how nomad families have kept a Boz Üy alive for generations.

What floor area does each size provide?
  • 5m (16 ft): ~19.6 m² (211 sq ft). A studio, a meditation room, an intimate stay.
  • 6m (20 ft): ~28.3 m² (304 sq ft). Room to live and sleep, or a yoga floor.
  • 7m (23 ft): ~38.5 m² (414 sq ft). A family, or a workshop.
  • 8m (26 ft): ~50.3 m² (541 sq ft). An event space, a resort's centrepiece.

Larger still by commission: pole-free to 10m, and 11 to 12m with discreet central support. And because nothing stands in the middle, every square metre is usable, which is not true of a ger.

What sizes and finishes do you offer?

Four sizes, named the Kyrgyz way:

  • Jailoo (5m): the summer high pasture. Light, close, intimate.
  • Kochuu (6m): the migration. Our most-ordered size, poised between snug and generous.
  • Ala-Too (7m): the mountain range. Built for presence.
  • 8m and larger: a centrepiece, to order.

Then one real choice, the finish. Ak is undyed felt in cream and stone: the clean, architectural reading we recommend for hospitality, where the form carries the room and nothing is lifted out of its context. Oyu is the fully dressed Boz Üy, shyrdak carpets, oimo chiy mats, zabık bash bands, a carved bosogo door, for those who want exactly that, and we build it gladly. For wet climates we add a breathable rain cover and extra insulation. Every order is priced on a call, against your spec and your destination.

Ordering & Pricing

How does the ordering process work?

It starts with a conversation, not a cart.

  • You write: the units, sizes, site and timeline, through the enquiry form or by email.
  • We talk: a video call, where we learn the project and settle the specification and its price.
  • Quote and deposit: a fixed quote, and a deposit that starts the build on a date we agree.
  • Made and checked: built by hand in Kyzyl-Tuu. Before anything ships you get a full QC pack, photographs, Adylet's video walkthrough, and the measurements.
  • Delivered and raised: we handle freight and customs, and our own team raises it on site.

Most orders run about six months from order to delivery.

Because there is no one price to publish. What a Boz Üy costs turns on its size, its finish, the export options your climate needs, where it is going, and how many you order. By international bank transfer (SWIFT/IBAN), quoted in USD, and tied to milestones: a deposit to begin, the balance before it ships. For multi-unit orders we write a staged schedule into the contract, matched to how the build and the shipping actually run.

  • the painting on the tündük

In the natural Ak finish the palette is deliberately narrow. We confirm the detail when we quote.

Shipping & Delivery

Where do you ship to?

Anywhere on Earth. What changes with distance is the freight cost and the lead time, so tell us where it is going and we fold both into the quote.

How long does shipping take?

Mostly by air, and here is why.

  • Air: about 10 to 14 days to most places, and how we ship the great majority of orders. Fast, and simpler through customs.
  • Ocean: possible for a very large order, but Kyrgyzstan is landlocked, so the crate first travels overland, by road or rail through China or the Central Asian corridors, to a seaport. That leg makes ocean slow and rarely any cheaper, so we usually advise against it.

Order to doorstep, the build is the long part, not the flight. Figure on roughly six months in all, with the air transit a week or two inside that.

What are the Incoterms, who handles customs clearance?

By default we quote DAP (Delivered at Place): freight, insurance and delivery to your address are in the price. Import duties and VAT on arrival are yours.

Every export document comes with it, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and the fumigation paperwork for the wooden crating (ISPM 15). We will also point you to the right HS codes so you can estimate your import duty before it lands.

Our own customs and shipping agent in Bishkek handles the Kyrgyz side and coordinates directly with your customs agent at the other end, so clearance is arranged on both sides before the shipment moves. It is experience that is hard to buy, and it is yours as part of the order.

If your team prefers to run its own forwarder, we ship FOB from the airport with the full papers and you carry it from there. Otherwise DAP, to your door, is how it goes.

How is the yurt packaged for shipping?

Packed by part and labelled: frame, felt, reed mats, door, fittings. Wood is film-wrapped, felt rolls sealed in moisture-resistant bags, every package numbered, photographed, and matched to a packing list you have before it leaves.

A 6m Boz Üy travels as six to nine packages, roughly 165 to 200 kg gross and 1.8 to 2.4 CBM.

What happens if something is damaged in transit?

Every shipment carries all-risk cargo insurance. If something arrives knocked about, photograph it before you sign for it and send us the pictures within 48 hours. We run the claim and replace what was hurt. And because the Boz Üy is built from replaceable parts, a damaged panel is a panel, not a whole new order.

Do I need to be present for delivery?

Be there, or send someone, the packages are heavy and worth inspecting the moment they arrive. The forwarder arranges a delivery window in advance. For larger orders to a business address, dock or forklift access helps.

Weather & Climate

Can a Kyrgyz yurt handle rain? Will the felt get wet?

Felt was made for the dry continental air of Central Asia. In a wet climate, the UK, the Pacific Northwest, much of Europe, wool takes on water and is slow to give it back: workable, but not what you want under a roof that stays up all year. So for those places we add a breathable rain cover, a membrane that sits over the felt and turns the rain while still letting the wool breathe and balance its own humidity.

We strongly recommend it anywhere past about 600mm of rain a year. It is standard in the export build, an add-on otherwise.

How cold can it get inside in winter?

Wool is one of the great natural insulators, and Kyrgyz families have wintered in these tents at minus thirty. Left unheated, though, the inside will in time meet the outside. For comfort through the year we recommend a modern heat pump: it warms and cools in one, runs quiet, and keeps open flame away from felt and willow. With that and extra insulation, a Boz Üy holds its comfort through most European and North American winters.

For genuinely brutal cold we build the roof in two layers of felt, which lifts its rating sharply. That adds weight up top, so we strengthen the frame to carry it, and we settle the felt build against your climate before we make it.

For winter living we recommend the export build: extra insulation and a heat pump sized to the room.
How does the yurt perform in strong wind?

The dome gives the wind almost nothing to push against, where a flat wall would catch it. Tension bands hold the frame, straps hold the felt, and the shape has stood against steppe gales for millennia. On a coast or an exposed ridge we have you anchor the base to the platform and peg the outer straps to the ground. The assembly guide shows exactly how.

Can yurts handle snow loads?

The curve sheds snow before it can pile up, and the dome carries the ordinary loads of a European or North American winter without fuss. Where the snow comes hard, keep the inside warm: a little heat reaching the roof stops ice forming around the tündük seal.

Is the yurt suitable for hot climates?

Yes, and the tündük is why. Open the ring and it works like a chimney: hot air lifts and leaves through the top, cool air draws in low through the walls. The room sits well below the outside heat, and wool insulates against the sun as surely as against the cold. Our Boz Üys stand in Mediterranean, Middle Eastern and Australian heat.

How do I care for and maintain the felt?

Felt asks little:

  • if it gets wet, let it dry right through, and air the inside
  • brush off dust with a soft brush before storing
  • keep the rolls somewhere dry if the Boz Üy stands idle a while
  • check once a year for moth, and treat with a natural repellent
  • replace a tired panel on its own, never the whole set

A full care guide comes with every order.

Assembly & Installation

How long does it take to assemble a yurt?

A complete home, raised in a day. Skilled hands can race one up far faster, but placing a Boz Üy well is not a race. Allow half a day to a day to raise it carefully and dress it right. The structure needs no tools; the components slot, pin, and tie together. We support your builders with a filmed build and live video, and for larger projects our own experts come to your site to raise the Boz Üys with your team.

Do I need a foundation or platform?

Traditionally a Boz Üy stands straight on cleared, level ground, and for a seasonal, movable use that still holds. For anything permanent it wants a proper foundation, and for a hospitality build that usually means a concrete pad or slab. It earns its place:

  • it keeps ground moisture off the frame
  • it gives you a level, clean floor
  • it insulates against cold rising from the earth
  • it carries the services and sits right with your local planning

The right base depends on your site and your use, and the base is only the start. We advise on the services, the drainage, where the bathroom sits (beside the Boz Üy, never inside it), and the layout, from our own experience, and we settle it all with you before anything is poured or laid. How we ready a site.

Do you provide an assembly guide and support?

Yes. Every order carries a full illustrated guide and a filmed walkthrough by Adylet, our master builder, with every component numbered to match. Your first raising comes with live video support at no charge, and on most projects our own team is there on site to do it with you.

Can the yurt be moved after assembly?

Yes, that is the whole idea. A Boz Üy comes down, travels, and goes back up as many times as you like, the way nomad families have moved them for centuries. Everything is numbered and packs the same way each time, so the second take-down is easier than the first.

Can I add electricity, plumbing, or heating?

Yes to all, and many run their Boz Üys as year-round lets or studios with full services:

  • Power: cabling usually runs under the platform and enters low through the kerege base. LED strip light around the kerege and tündük is a favourite.
  • Heating and cooling: a heat pump is the efficient answer, one unit for both, and it keeps open flame out of the room. Underfloor heating in the platform is popular too.
  • Water: rarely inside the Boz Üy itself, but a bathroom built alongside is a common arrangement.
How many people does a 6m yurt sleep?

A 6m Boz Üy (28.3 m²) sleeps two to four as a guest room, with a double or king bed, a corner to sit, and storage. Laid out the Kyrgyz way, with mats on the floor, it takes a family of six. As a yoga or meditation room it holds eight to twelve.

Planning & Permits

Do I need planning permission to put up a yurt?

It varies, by country, region and use, and we will not pretend otherwise. What usually decides it: whether the land is yours, whether the use is private or commercial, how long the Boz Üy stays up, and whether your authority counts it temporary or permanent.

A rough map, always to be checked with your local authority:

  • UK: temporary structures on private land for up to 28 days typically do not require planning permission. Permanent or commercial holiday-let installations often require consent.
  • US: varies by state and county. Many rural and agricultural zones permit yurts without a permit below a certain floor area.
  • EU: varies by country and municipality. Germany and France tend to require permits for structures above ~20 m².
  • Australia and NZ: similar to the EU, check with your local council.
We maintain a country-by-country guidance document and include the relevant section in your quote pack.
Can the yurt be used for commercial holiday lets or short-term rental?

Yes, it is one of the main things our Boz Üys are bought for. Clients run them as lets, eco-resorts and short stays. For commercial use we recommend the export build, the rain cover and climate control. Check your local hospitality licensing and fire-safety rules too, they differ by place, and we will help you read them for your location.

Can a Boz Üy be used for glamping?

Yes. A Boz Üy makes a glamping unit that nothing in a catalogue can match: a real UNESCO-protected Kyrgyz yurt, not a factory-stitched canvas tent dressed up to look like one. For a glampsite or boutique resort we finish it to a luxury-suite standard, add the breathable rain cover and insulation for your climate, and deliver at the volume you need. A guest who walks into the genuine article knows the difference, and pays for it. See yurts for glamping and hospitality for how we build and deliver at scale.

What should I know about fire safety?

Felt is a natural wool textile, not a treated flame-retardant fabric, and we make no fire-rating claims. For any commercial or guest use, follow your local fire-safety requirements and consult the relevant authority. We can provide material and component details for your assessment, and we never recommend leaving open flames unattended inside any fabric structure.

Can I get building or structural certification for a Tündük yurt?

We give you a data pack, every component dimension, the materials, and how it goes together. The formal certification a permit needs has to come from a licensed engineer in your own jurisdiction, not from us. Where trade clients have had a Boz Üy certified locally, we have supplied the documentation they needed for it. Tell us what your project requires.

How do I work out what size yurt I need?

A place to start:

  • A retreat for one or two, a garden studio: 5m (Jailoo)
  • A guest room, or a yoga floor for 8 to 12: 6m (Kochuu)
  • A family let, a workshop, a small event: 7m (Ala-Too)
  • A resort centrepiece, a large event: 8m, and larger by commission

And remember there is no dead space. Nothing stands in the middle, so a 6m Boz Üy's 28 m² is 28 m² of open floor.

Kyrgyz Terminology

What does "tündük" mean, and why is it your name?

The tündük is the crown, the wooden hoop at the top of the Boz Üy that every roof pole runs into and radiates down from. It holds the whole dome together. It also holds the meaning: the sun, the cosmos, the family gathered beneath it.

It matters enough to Kyrgyz identity that it sits at the centre of the national flag, a yellow tündük on a red field. We took the name because the tündük is at once the most structural part and the most sacred. Everything radiates from it. So do we.

What is a "kerege", and what does "6 kanat" mean?

The kerege is the wall: a folding lattice of wood, accordion-like, that opens out to make the cylinder the Boz Üy stands on. Each panel folds flat to travel and expands to its arc on site.

Kanat means "wing", and it is how Kyrgyz builders have counted size for centuries. A "6 kanat" Boz Üy has six kerege panels round its wall. Ours follow the old count: 5m is 4 kanat, 6m is 6, 7m is 8. The roof poles scale with it, 55 uuk at 4 kanat, 85 at 6, 100 at 8. When we say "6 kanat", we are speaking the builder's own language.

What are "uuk", how are they different from regular rafters?

Uuk are the roof poles, the ones that curve from the tündük down to the top of the walls to make the dome. A modern Western yurt uses straight rafters. Ours are bent, steamed from Turpan Tal willow over 15 to 20 days until each one holds its arc for good, with nothing mechanical to keep it there.

That curve is the reason there is no centre pole. The dome holds itself: the uuk press out against the kerege and up into the tündük, and the whole thing stands in compression. A 6m Boz Üy carries 85 of them, every one cut and bent by hand.

What is "Turpan Tal" and why does it matter?

Turpan Tal is the specific species of willow used for all Kyrgyz yurt frames, harvested from river valleys in the Fergana and Talas regions. It is exceptionally light for its strength, naturally flexible when steamed, and resistant to splitting under tension once dried.

Not all willow is Turpan Tal. The species is selected specifically because it bends the way the uuk and kerege need to bend, holding a curve without cracking when the fibres are stressed by steam. Builders in Kyzyl-Tuu have used Turpan Tal for generations. Any other species produces a structurally inferior frame. It is one of the reasons authentic Kyrgyz yurts from the right villages are genuinely different from imitations.

What is "kiyiz", is it the same as felt?

Kiyiz is the Kyrgyz word for felt, the handmade kind, and it is nothing like the industrial sort. You wash raw sheep wool, lay it on a reed mat, drench it in hot water, then roll and press it, again and again, a full day to a panel. It is work done together, because the rolling needs several pairs of hands moving in rhythm.

A 6m Boz Üy takes thirteen rolls of it, eight for the roof, five for the walls, 2 to 4cm thick. It is the insulation, the weatherproofing and the skin all at once. And unlike any synthetic cover, kiyiz breathes with the weather: it draws in moisture when the air is damp and lets it go as the air dries.

What is "gök" binding, why no nails?

Gök is rawhide, strips of untanned animal hide applied wet to every joint in the kerege lattice. As rawhide dries, it contracts and hardens, creating a joint of extraordinary compressive strength. There are no nails, no screws, no metal fasteners of any kind in an authentic Kyrgyz yurt frame.

This is not a limitation, it is a structural advantage. Gök-bound joints flex under wind load and movement rather than cracking under stress the way rigid metal-fastened joints do. The yurt is designed to move slightly, distributing force across the entire structure rather than concentrating it at fixed points. This is why traditional yurts survive the extreme wind conditions of Kyrgyz mountain passes.

What is a "shyrdak", how is it different from "ala kiyiz"?

Both are handmade Kyrgyz felt carpets, made using completely different techniques.

Shyrdak is made by the mosaic appliqué method: two contrasting-colour felt layers are cut simultaneously with the same pattern, then the cut-out pieces are swapped and stitched together. The design is first drawn on the felt, then cut, then reassembled. Every motif is intentional and carries meaning, the ram's horn (strength), the crow's claw (lineage), the horn scroll (protection).

Ala kiyiz is older and made by the rolling method: coloured wool designs are laid into raw, unfelted wool, and then the entire surface is wet-rolled together, permanently fusing the design into the fabric as the fibres bond. Unlike shyrdak, you cannot plan an ala kiyiz on paper; the entire design must exist in the maker's memory before the wool is touched, because once rolling begins, the pattern cannot be altered.

What are "chiy" and "oimo chiy"?

Chiy are woven reed mats that wrap around the kerege walls beneath the felt covering. They serve as an essential insulation and ventilation layer, separating the felt from direct contact with the lattice, creating an air gap, and providing additional thermal mass. In a properly built Boz Üy, you never skip the chiy layer.

Oimo chiy is the premium version: during the weaving process, coloured wool is woven into the reed surface in geometric patterns. The decoration is not applied afterward, it is integral to the structure. Plain chiy serves function; oimo chiy serves function and beauty at once. It lines the interior of the kerege and is visible as a decorative band around the lower walls.

What are "zabık bash" and "bosogo"?

Zabık bash are the decorative felt bands that ring the yurt at the wall-to-roof junction. The ichki (inner) zabık bash lines the interior where the kerege meets the uuk, an embroidered felt strip with traditional motifs in red, green, and gold. The syrtky (outer) zabık bash runs around the exterior lower edge. Together they are the finishing detail that transforms a functional yurt into a fully dressed one. A yurt without zabık bash is like a suit without its buttons.

Bosogo is the wooden door frame, typically the most visually prominent element of the yurt exterior. In the natural Ak finish the bosogo is plain-painted; in the decorated Oyu finish it is hand-carved with ancestral motifs and painted in traditional colours by the master craftsman. The bosogo is what a guest sees first when approaching the yurt.

What do Jailoo, Kochuu, and Ala-Too mean, and what are Ak and Oyu?

Our three sizes are named for Kyrgyz words that carry meaning relevant to how and where each yurt lives:

  • Jailoo (5m): the summer alpine pasture, where nomadic families have pitched their yurts every July and August for thousands of years. Light, intimate, made for retreat.
  • Kochuu (6m): the migration, the act of moving your home to follow the seasons. Our most-ordered size. Balanced between intimacy and generosity.
  • Ala-Too (7m): the mountain range that rings Bishkek and defines the Kyrgyz skyline. Grand, commanding, built for permanence.

Within each size, the two finishes are:

  • Ak (white, pure): natural undyed kiyiz felt in cream and stone tones. No chemical treatment. The traditional palette of the steppe.
  • Oyu (motif, ornament): fully decorated with shyrdak carpets, oimo chiy reed mats, zabık bash felt bands, and a carved bosogo door frame. Everything handmade by the women and craftsmen of Kyzyl-Tuu.

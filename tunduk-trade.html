Spec 01

Certified for guest occupancy

Fire-retardant textiles certified to the standard each market requires, ASTM E84 for US occupancy, NFPA 701 and CPAI-84 for the fabrics themselves, Eurocode equivalents across the EU. Certification is part of every hospitality build, not an afterthought.

Spec 02

All-season specification

Insulated for desert heat and mountain cold, with structural calculations benchmarked to local wind and snow loads. The Boz Üy has held the steppe for thousands of years. We engineer it for your site and your code. For climate control, we recommend air conditioning over a wood stove, for efficiency and fire safety.

Spec 03

Serviceable for the long run

A wood frame that lasts decades, with replacement felt and covers held for you and a clear maintenance schedule. Your asset stays beautiful through years of occupancy, not just the opening-week photographs.

Spec 04

In every order

What arrives on your site.

The frame

The tündük crown ring, the curved uuk roof poles, the kerege lattice walls, and the bosogo door frame with its door. Steam-bent willow, rawhide-bound, no metal.

The felt skin

Handmade wool felt for the roof and the walls, over a woven reed (chiy) insulation layer. Carded, rolled, and sewn by hand in the village.

The weather cover

A breathable weatherproof outer cover for wet climates, repelling rain while letting the wool breathe. Not a plastic tarp over the craft.

The interior, your call

By default, the clean architectural fit-out we recommend for hospitality: natural felt and considered, modern detail. Or, for those who want the authentic article, the fully decorated traditional version, shyrdak and zabık bash and all.

The spares for assembly

Additional roof poles and the central raising prop (bakan) used to lift the crown, so the first assembly goes up without improvisation.

The documentation

An illustrated assembly guide, Adylet's recorded QC walkthrough of your actual unit, and the full export papers your freight needs.

The people behind it

Not a factory. A craft, scaled with care.

Tündük is led by Yossi Ghinsberg, who has lived in tents on four continents since 1995, and Nura, a Kyrgyz keeper of the tradition who runs the family's Boz Üy camp on Lake Issyk-Kul.

Behind every frame stands Adylet, our master builder, and the family workshop he leads in Kyzyl-Tuu, the village that is the world centre of the craft. We work inside that relationship. It is what no rival can source on a price list.

It is why we can promise authenticity and volume in the same breath. The craft is real. The people are real. We have simply built the bridge that carries it to your property.

The master builder

Every Boz Üy passes through Adylet's hands.

Adylet is a third-generation master builder. He learned the craft from his father, who learned it from his. His family workshop in Kyzyl-Tuu was building yurts before the Soviet cooperative that formalised the trade, kept building through it, and builds still.

He does not work alone. The workshop is a family of specialists, each holding a part of the craft, which is how a single village answers an order at volume without thinning what makes each one real.

Personal QC · every shipment

Before a single unit leaves Kyrgyzstan, Adylet records a full video walkthrough of it, every component named, every measurement confirmed. It is how we are certain that what arrives on the far side of the world is exactly what was built in Kyzyl-Tuu.
